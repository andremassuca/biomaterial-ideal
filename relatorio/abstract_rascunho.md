# Abstract

**Introduction:** Selecting the material of an implant requires balancing mechanical, biological, clinical and economic requirements that often conflict, and no single material is ideal for every application.

**Methods:** Target-based multi-criteria decision-making (TOPSIS, WASPAS and VIKOR) was applied to material selection for a femoral stem, a coronary stent and a bone scaffold, with a semi-quantitative analysis of the hip bearing couple and a dental implant. The most influential values were verified in primary sources, with explicit rules for product form and porosity; the exception is three platinum-chromium properties of the stent, so far confirmed only in secondary sources. Robustness was assessed by Monte Carlo simulation and sensitivity scenarios, including a stronger weight on the criteria linked to each device's critical problem and a budget ceiling. The implementation was verified by reproducing a published study.

**Results:** For the femoral stem, Ti-6Al-4V ELI ranked first with all three methods, in both scenarios and, in scenario A, in at least 9,999 of the 10,000 Monte Carlo repetitions. For the bearing couple, ceramic-on-ceramic (ZTA/ZTA) obtained the best score in the semi-quantitative analysis focused on wear. For the stent, platinum-chromium and cobalt-chromium L605 were very close (each alloy ranks first with at least one method, and the order depends on the scenario); platinum-chromium ranked first more often in the reference Monte Carlo simulation, but VIKOR does not separate it from L605 and WASPAS prefers L605; the proposal is: Pt-Cr and L605 are practically equivalent; Pt-Cr is favoured by TOPSIS, VIKOR and radiopacity, L605 by WASPAS and by having all its properties in supplier data sheets. There is no universal winner. For the scaffold, the answer depends on load: the 3D-printed PCL/β-TCP composite ranked first in the deterministic result in 5 of the 6 method and scenario combinations, but porous β-TCP ranked first more often in the data Monte Carlo simulation with all three methods and was favoured when only cell support and degradation were prioritised. For the dental implant, commercially pure grade 4 titanium obtained the best score in every version of the semi-quantitative analysis. Data verification changed the scaffold result twice (on 1 and 11 October 2026), and reproducing the published study revealed an inconsistency between its data and its reported results.

**Conclusion:** A multi-criteria ranking is no more reliable than the data, weights and assumptions behind it. Rather than identifying a universally ideal biomaterial, this work proposes a traceable process to separate stable choices from those that depend on uncertain data or specific priorities.

**Keywords:** biomaterials; multi-criteria decision-making; target-based criteria; robustness analysis; hip prosthesis; coronary stent; bone scaffold; dental implant.
